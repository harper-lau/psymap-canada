Method & limitations

A clearer path from “I wonder why” to “this is Psychology.”

Psychology Map Canada is an educational map for curiosity. It helps students discover academic fields, schools, professor research pathways, learning opportunities, and source trails connected to questions about human behaviour.

“AI proposes. Humans verify. Sources decide.”

01

Why this map

A student can be fascinated by memory, social pressure, emotions, language, or identity without knowing which psychology terms to search. Psychology Map Canada begins with the student's words and makes the surrounding academic landscape easier to see.

The goal is orientation, not an automated answer. A useful map should help a learner form better questions and reach sources they can inspect for themselves.

02

The problem

University information is rich but often organized for people who already know a department's terminology. Research profiles, labs, programs, and course descriptions can live on separate pages and use different classification systems.

The map connects those pieces while keeping a clear boundary: the interest activity is not a psychological test, ranking evidence is never blended into a made-up score, and no pathway is an admissions promise.

03

Search & scoring

Global search normalizes case and punctuation, checks school names, abbreviations, aliases, professor names, and fields, then applies lightweight edit-distance matching so a near miss such as “UVD” can still surface UBC. Results are ordered by deterministic relevance rules.

The ten-question interest explorer converts each 1–5 response to a 0–4 value, multiplies it by the visible field weights, and scales each field independently from 0 to 100. The percentages do not need to total 100 and do not measure ability or suitability.

04

Research process

The intended process separates discovery from evidence. Tools may help propose search language, candidate connections, or records to investigate. A person must then inspect authoritative university sources, confirm what each source supports, and decide whether the information belongs in Psychology Map Canada.

Propose→Investigate→Verify→Publish with sources

05

Verification

Official university, department, course-catalogue, and professional-organization pages are preferred for program and research facts. Every real professor record points to a public source, states when it was checked, and keeps official research language distinct from the map's student-friendly field labels.

The current directory contains 36 selected profiles across UBC, SFU, UVic, the University of Toronto, McGill, the University of Alberta, the University of Calgary, and Dalhousie. Records were checked on September 4 or September 8, 2026, as shown on each profile. Names, roles, public emails, profile links, and summarized research focus are tied to official university profiles; Psychology Map field connections and student questions are editorial mappings.

06

Limitations

  • The directory includes 20 school scaffolds. UBC and SFU have limited source-backed program context; six additional universities currently have selected verified professor profiles but program-level research remains pending.
  • The 36 professor profiles are not complete faculty lists. The six lab cards and ten course cards remain explicitly fictional or representative development data.
  • Methods or populations are shown only when summarized from the checked profile; otherwise they remain unavailable.
  • No comparable ranking series is currently published; all ranking cells therefore remain N/A.
  • Fuzzy search can surface helpful near matches, but it cannot understand every intention or ambiguous phrase.
  • Field matches are browsing prompts, not conclusions, advice, or measures of fit.
  • Official information can change; students must re-check linked sources.

07

What comes next

Evidence collection continues

Future work should expand verified professor coverage, collect current English-language courses, confirm department size and laboratories, and add comparable third-party ranking series before replacing remaining unavailable or sample states.
